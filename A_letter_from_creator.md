## What Is Cronoscope Trying to Become?

The long-term goal is ambitious.

I want to take the vast and unique wavelength measurements from NASA's SPHEREx mission, combine them with observations from other astronomical archives, and explore how different datasets and images can be brought together to create a more cohesive picture of the universe across time and space.

Not just where something is.

But what we're observing, at what wavelengths, when it was observed, and how those observations relate to one another.

That's the long game. But what else is time for?

## What Have We Built So Far?

We've been developing ways to explore wavelength measurements across observation timescales, investigate moving-object candidates, and display scientific data in ways that make it easier to understand visually.

We've built and tested pipelines for working with astronomical observations, image data, coordinate systems, and ephemerides.

And we've spent a ridiculous amount of time making sure the measurements, transformations, and visualizations are grounded in actual data rather than something that just looks impressive on a screen.

It's still a work in progress.

Some techniques are verified. Others remain experimental. And there's plenty left to figure out.

But that's part of the point.

## Where Do We Go From Here?

In the near future, I want to expand the visualization tools, improve how wavelength information is represented through color overlays, and continue integrating additional astronomical archives, including MAST, GAIA, and more.

Eventually, I want Cronoscope to let someone explore multiple observations, wavelengths, and datasets together, moving through both space and time without needing to be an astrophysicist to understand what they're looking at.

I want someone to be able to see the science, interact with it, question it, and maybe discover something they didn't expect.

Because that's exactly how this project started.

One question led to another.

One problem became ten smaller problems.

And somewhere between curiosity, frustration, research, debugging, and a whole lot of AI-assisted code, Cronoscope started becoming something real.

I don't know how far this project will go.

But I know why I started it.
